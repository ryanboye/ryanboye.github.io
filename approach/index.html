The role of Design

Design is a horizontal function that cuts across product lines.

Because designers see customer pain points and the lifecycle end to end, they can think across all of the products and services a customer interacts with. They do not need to feel tied down by the gaps between product lines. This allows the business to flexibly focus its efforts on specific product problems in a vertical way while maintaining visibility of the customer journey from end to end.

Why Design should cut across.

01

See the whole journey.

Designers work across product silos by nature. That puts us in a position to catch the patterns, gaps, and contradictions that no single vertical team is positioned to see.

02

Productize customer opportunities.

Customer understanding becomes valuable when it turns into a point of view about what should be built, how the pieces should fit together, and which opportunity matters most.

03

Give teams shared context.

Design helps focused product teams understand how their decisions affect the wider experience—without asking the business to abandon the vertical ownership that lets it move quickly.

Leadership

How I lead Design

I build teams with clear expectations, meaningful ownership, and a high bar for craft. My passion is teaching designers to advocate for their point of view, recognize opportunities across the customer journey, and turn those opportunities into products worth building.

1

A clear career framework for growth

A clear framework is essential to the growth of high-performing, career-oriented designers. We want to make sure the company is a place where hungry talent can thrive. Alongside Unity's other design leaders, I helped create the company-wide framework used to level designers and make expectations, advancement, and what came next explicit.

2

Massive ownership.

I give designers meaningful ownership and room to take real risks, then support them in making the work successful. I stay technical enough to get into the details and hands-on when needed. My feedback is candid and specific, and the quality bar is high.

I stay deeply involved in product decisions and guide work such as the Unity.com redesign, while giving designers ownership of their areas.

3

Teach designers to turn insight into influence.

Designers should be able to articulate an opportunity, not only present an output. I coach them to connect customer pain to business value, frame a product thesis, and advocate for what should be built.

That growth creates senior designers who can operate across the journey, identify gaps between roadmaps, and lead alongside Product and Engineering.
